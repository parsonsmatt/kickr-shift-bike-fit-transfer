Fit bike constants — where the scales sit and which way they move
Each carriage is a zero mark plus two slides. Give where the zero mark sits relative to the bottom bracket with standover at A, then describe each slide. Standover B through H moves both horizontal reach/setback masts 20 mm per letter along an axis that leans 4° back, so each letter is mostly up but slightly rearward. The height slide takes a tube angle, read the way you would read a seat tube or head tube angle: 73 leans back, 90 is vertical. The fore/aft and reach slides are near level, so they take a tilt off horizontal: 0 is level, +3 rises 3°, −3 drops 3°. Which way along a slide a bigger reading travels is carried by the sign of its mm-per-unit, so use a negative gain if the scale counts the other way. Every field prints back what it means in words — if that sentence disagrees with the bike in front of you, the number is wrong. The values below are measured off the bike; section 2 can re-solve them from a bike you already know.

2 — Calibrate from a bike you already know

Rather than measuring the fit bike, set it up to match a bike whose build you know and note the standover position and scale readings. The app works out where those two points sit from that frame's geometry and its actual stem, spacers and seatpost, then back-solves the constants above. One reference fixes the zero positions. Two or more also fit the travel per unit and report how well everything agrees.

6 — Reverse: set the fit bike up to match a real bike

The other direction. Fill in the highlighted frame's as built figures in section 3 — its actual stem, spacers, saddle height and rail offset — and this says what to set the fit bike to so you sit in the same place. Standover shifts both carriages together, so every position below is an exact answer and none is more correct than another: pick whichever keeps all four readings on the scales. Readings are rounded to the millimetre, as the scales are marked, so the round trip lands within about 1 mm rather than exactly. Click a frame card or a compare row to switch which frame this reads.

Assumptions worth checking

Standover rise. Positions A through H translate both horizontal reach/setback masts along a single axis that leans back from vertical. A is the reference; each following letter adds 20 mm measured along that axis, through H at 140 mm. At the 4° lean entered above that works out as 19.95 mm up and 1.40 mm back per letter, so A to H is 139.7 mm up and 9.8 mm back. The rearward part is small per letter but reaches roughly 10 mm at H, which is more than the match tolerance — if the axis is actually vertical, set the lean to 0.

Headset stack. Frame stack is measured to the top of the head tube, but spacers start on top of the upper headset cover. That cover is 5–20 mm depending on the frame and it moves the answer by exactly its own height. It is per-frame and defaults to 15 mm.

Stem stack height. Stem angle is measured from perpendicular to the steerer, and the stem's clamp is roughly 40 mm tall, so half of it is added above the spacers to reach the bar clamp centreline.

Saddle height across seat angles. The same saddle position produces a different saddle height number on a frame with a different seat angle. The per-frame figure is recomputed along that frame's own axis rather than carried across.

Saddle. The fit bike's saddle carriage locates the rail clamp, so run the saddle you actually ride. Nose-to-rail and rail drop only affect the drawing and the reported saddle height, not the seatpost answer.
